Structural & Thermal Qualification

Virtual environmental qualification of a machined avionics enclosure for a 7 in class long-range multirotor. Every requirement traces to a manufacturer-published limit, a military standard, or a platform-derived excitation condition. Verification is by analysis only — no physical test article is produced.

Closed assembly. 93 × 63 × 33 mm body, 79 mm across the mounting tabs. Six-fastener lid, four mounting tabs, and a side cable penetration for the external GPS and receiver harness.

Lid removed. Standoffs on the 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack pattern, and a divider rib separating the board stack from the bay holding the video transmitter and the upright 6S bulk capacitor.

Status — structural analysis complete, thermal redesign open. Modal FEA is done and both structural frequency requirements pass. The thermal figures below come from a lumped-parameter hand calculation, not FEA — thermal FEA has not been run, and no CFD has been performed on this project. Random vibration, the MIL-STD-810H PSD profile, fatigue, and the Phase 5 thermal redesign all remain open.

Design finding — the enclosure is a Faraday cage. The GPS module uses an internal patch antenna and the receiver an integrated SMD ceramic antenna. Neither would acquire signal inside an aluminium box, so both are mounted externally and excluded from the internal thermal model. Two thermal requirements were deleted as a direct consequence. This came out of the requirements work, before any geometry existed.
Two temperature limits that must not be conflated. The flight controller carries a 40 °C maximum ambient rating alongside a ~100 °C component rating. Inside a sealed enclosure the ambient limit is the one that governs internal air, and it is far more restrictive. The binding component allowable is 90 °C, set by the video transmitter case.

The ESC has no output BEC, so all 5 V loads draw from the flight controller's onboard regulator and the conversion losses dissipate on C1 — which is why its dissipation is booked at 2.00 W rather than its published 1.0 W static figure. The ESC's larger 46 × 44 mm outline, not the flight controller, sets the minimum internal footprint.

The separation factor is a judgement call, and it is stated as one. A 1.5× factor on the fourth harmonic at maximum RPM gives the 3,440 Hz target — a common preliminary-design rule for avionics mounting structure. A 2.0× factor would raise the target to 4,587 Hz and the design would not close. That is recorded as a sensitivity rather than a failure, because the bonded-contact assumption already over-predicts stiffness; tightening one while leaving the other optimistic would not be a meaningful test.

Modal FEA

Ten modes extracted in Fusion 360 (Nastran), bonded contacts throughout, fixed on the four mounting tab underside faces.

Every mode clears the band. The four prop harmonics at maximum RPM span 87–2,293 Hz. The first mode sits at 3,984 Hz — above the 3,440 Hz requirement and clear of the fourth harmonic by a factor of 1.74.

Mode 1 does not converge — and the pattern says why. Its shift grew from 3.23% to 3.96% across successive refinements rather than shrinking, and Richardson extrapolation returns a negative observed order of convergence: the discretisation error is not reducing monotonically. Modes 3 and 4 converge cleanly at 0.81% and 0.17% — and both are dominated by the base, a single solid body with no contact dependence. Every poorly converging mode (1, 2, 5, 8, 10) involves the lid or the capacitor, both of which depend on bonded contact between separately meshed bodies. Contact discretisation at those interfaces is the probable cause, not global mesh density.
The reported first mode is the lowest of the three. At 3,983.7 Hz the 5% mesh gives the most conservative available estimate, which is what the +0.16 margin is built on. That margin should be read with the convergence caveat attached.

Thermal Scoping

A lumped-parameter hand calculation in Python, built deliberately before any FEA — to establish an expected answer to check the FEA against.

What this calculation is. Churchill-Chu natural convection, flat-plate laminar forced convection, and linearised radiation, solved by fixed-point iteration for a single isothermal surface temperature. It does not resolve internal gradients, component hot spots, or conduction through mounts — those need the thermal FEA, which has not been run. This is not CFD, and no CFD has been performed on this project. Because the enclosure is treated as isothermal, internal air will run hotter than these numbers: treat them as optimistic.

The sealed architecture fails REQ-THM-001 in every case. Hover is the binding condition: it has the worst heat rejection despite the lowest ESC load. Anodising helps materially at hover — radiation carries 6.3 of the 14.3 W/m²·K total against 0.8 for bare aluminium — which makes surface finish a first-order design variable rather than a detail. It is not close to enough.

Ruling out the obvious fix

Closing a 34 °C rise against a 5 °C allowable requires 9.2× the current external area. The obvious response is fins, so fins were sized rather than assumed:

Can fins close the gap?
ApproachArea achievedArea requiredMass addedVerdict
Densely finned long walls3.4×9.2×+101 gInsufficient
Relocate the 7.9 W VTX externally—2.5×−12 gAchievable
Fins reach 3.4× while adding 101 g — more than doubling the 96 g base — and still fall short of 9.2×. That result is what redirects the redesign from incremental surface area to architecture. Moving the video transmitter outside removes 7.9 W of the 10.8 W internal load and drops the requirement to 2.5×, which fins can deliver. The redesign target changed because the arithmetic ruled out the alternative, not because the alternative looked unattractive.

Assumptions Log

Every assumption made in place of a sourced value, recorded with its justification and its direction of conservatism — including the ones that do not favour the design.

Assumptions and their direction of conservatism
#AssumptionJustificationImpact if wrong
A1C1 dissipation taken as 2.00 WPublished static power is 1.0 W; rounded up to bound BEC conversion lossesOver-predicts heat load by ~0.6 W. Conservative.
A2C2 dissipation taken as 0.90 W at hoverConduction loss scales as I²·Rds(on)Loss scales with the square of current — a 2× current error is a 4× dissipation error
A3Ambient temperature 35 °CHot-day condition for the intended operating environmentDirectly offsets every predicted temperature
A4C1 40 °C limit read as max ambientDistinct from the ~100 °C component rating; inside a sealed box the ambient limit governsIf it is a component limit, REQ-THM-001 is over-conservative
A5Standoffs modelled as aluminiumStiffer than the nylon commonly used in FPV buildsNylon would lower the board stack modes. NON-CONSERVATIVE.
A6All interfaces modelled as bondedRepresents fully preloaded bolted joints with no slipOver-predicts assembly stiffness and therefore natural frequencies. NON-CONSERVATIVE for REQ-STR-001.
A7Components modelled as homogeneous solidsCorrect mass and envelope; FEA needs mass distribution, not board layoutLocal board stiffness unrepresented. Affects board-level modes only.
Two assumptions work against the design, and both touch the one requirement that passes. Bonded contacts (A6) over-predict stiffness, and aluminium standoffs (A5) are stiffer than the nylon a real build would likely use. Both inflate natural frequencies, which is precisely the direction that makes REQ-STR-001 easier to satisfy. The +0.16 margin is therefore an upper bound on the true margin — the same reason a 2.0× separation factor was not adopted while these assumptions stand.
09

Open Items

What Phase 5 and beyond must close.

Phase 5 thermal redesign

Relocate the video transmitter, fin to the 2.5× target, and re-run. This is the item that decides whether the design closes at all.

Thermal FEA

Resolve internal gradients and component hot spots the lumped model cannot see. If the FEA disagrees with the hand calculation by more than ~30%, that gap needs explaining before either result is trusted.

Random vibration & fatigue

Miles' equation for 3σ response, then static FEA against 6061-T6 yield, plus a fatigue hand calculation on the mounting interface. REQ-STR-003 and 004 are open.

MIL-STD-810H profile

Obtain Method 514.8 and extract the PSD profile with a stated rationale for why it fits a small multirotor. The excitation band is platform-derived only.

Datasheet gaps

Confirm whether the C1 40 °C figure is ambient or component, state the assumed ESC phase current for both flight conditions, confirm the VTX runs from VBAT, and obtain the capacitor temperature rating.

Mass budget

REQ-MAS-001 has no allocation yet. The base is 96 g; a finned redesign would add materially to that, so the budget needs setting before the redesign is sized.
