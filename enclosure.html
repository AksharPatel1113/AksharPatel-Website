UAS Avionics Enclosure
Structural & Thermal Qualification

Virtual environmental qualification of a machined avionics enclosure for a 7" class long-range multirotor. Requirements traced to manufacturer datasheets, modal FEA against a prop-passing excitation band, and a preliminary thermal hand calculation that rules the sealed architecture out before any FEA was built.

Fusion 360 Nastran Modal FEA Python 6061-T6 MIL-STD-810H Requirements Engineering Trade Study

Closed assembly. 93 × 63 × 33 mm body, 79 mm across the mounting tabs. Six-fastener lid, four mounting tabs, and a side antenna penetration.

Lid removed. Internal mounting bosses on the 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack pattern, a divider rib separating the stack bay from the capacitor and VTX bay.

Project status — phases 1 to 4 complete, phase 5 in work. Requirements, trade study, CAD, and modal FEA are done. The thermal figures below are a lumped-parameter hand calculation, not FEA — thermal FEA is outstanding. No CFD has been run on this project. Random vibration, the MIL-STD-810H PSD profile, and the phase 5 redesign remain open.

01

Requirements Baseline

Verification is by analysis only — no physical test article. Every requirement traces to a manufacturer-published limit, a military standard, or a platform-derived excitation condition.

Component selection by weighted trade study

One component sets the thermal problem. The video transmitter dissipates 7.9 W — more than the rest of the stack combined. Dissipation is DC input power minus RF output power, so a 1.6 W transmitter drawing 9.5 W turns 7.9 W into localised heat. Every alternative evaluated was worse: the DJI O3 and O4 Air Units dissipate 11 W and 12.5 W and both require forced air.
Design finding — the enclosure is a Faraday cage. An aluminium box attenuates the signals of any component with an integrated antenna. The GPS module and RC receiver were therefore relocated externally rather than packaged inside. This came out of the requirements work, before any geometry existed.

Excitation band from the propulsion system

The motor/prop combination does not sit inside the enclosure — it is selected because it defines the vibration environment. Prop passing frequency is f = (RPM / 60) × Nblades, evaluated across four harmonics for the selected T-Motor F90 2806.5 running a 7" bi-blade at 2,600–17,200 RPM.

The 3,440 Hz requirement is 1.5× the highest excitation frequency — a common preliminary separation rule. The binding thermal allowable is the flight controller's 40 °C ambient rating, which is a distinct and far more restrictive number than its ~100 °C component rating. Inside a sealed box, the ambient limit governs.

03

Modal FEA

10 modes extracted in Fusion 360 (Nastran), bonded contacts throughout, fixed on the four mounting tab underside faces.

REQ-STR-001 / REQ-STR-002 verification
RequirementPredictedAllowableMarginStatus
REQ-STR-001 — first mode above 3,440 Hz3,984 Hz3,440 Hz+0.16PASS
REQ-STR-002 — no mode in excitation band3,984 Hz (f₁)87–2,293 HzNo mode in bandPASS

The first mode is lid drumming — a first bending shape with a central antinode on the lid. Higher modes progress from lid deformation, through bending of the internal stack about each axis, to coupled modes where the capacitor and video transmitter drive wall deformation. The lid is the softest element in the assembly, which is what makes the contact treatment at the lid interface consequential.

Mesh convergence — and where it did not converge

Three meshes were run at 10%, 7%, and 5% element size. The result is worth stating precisely rather than summarising as “converged”:

Three-mesh convergence study
ModeChange 10% → 7%Change 7% → 5%Assessment
Mode 13.2 %4.0 %Not converged — change grew
Mode 3—0.8 %Converged
Mode 4—0.2 %Converged
Mode 1 did not converge, and the other modes localise the cause. A refinement that reduces error should make successive changes smaller; here mode 1 moved 3.2% then 4.0% — it got worse. Modes 3 and 4 converged cleanly at 0.8% and 0.2% on the same meshes, so the mesh itself is adequate. What separates mode 1 is that it is a lid-dominated shape, and the lid is where the bonded contact discretisation lives. The convergence failure is therefore attributed to contact discretisation at the lid interface, not to global mesh density. The +0.16 margin should be read with that caveat attached.
04

Thermal Scoping

A lumped-parameter hand calculation in Python, built deliberately before any FEA — to establish an expected answer to check the FEA against.

What this calculation is. Churchill-Chu natural convection, flat-plate laminar forced convection, and linearised radiation, solved by fixed-point iteration for a single isothermal surface temperature. It does not resolve internal gradients, component hot spots, or conduction through mounts — those need the thermal FEA, which has not yet been run. This is not CFD, and no CFD has been performed on this project. Because the enclosure is treated as isothermal, internal air will run hotter than these numbers: treat them as optimistic.

The sealed architecture fails REQ-THM-001 in every case. Hover is the binding condition: it has the worst heat rejection despite the lowest ESC load. Anodising helps materially at hover — radiation carries 6.3 of the 14.3 W/m²·K total against 0.8 for bare aluminium — which makes surface finish a first-order design variable rather than a detail. It is not close to enough.

Ruling out the obvious fix

Closing a 34 °C rise against a 5 °C allowable requires 9.2× the current external area. The obvious response is fins, so fins were sized rather than assumed:

Can fins close the gap?
ApproachArea achievedArea requiredMass addedVerdict
Densely finned long walls3.4×9.2×+101 gInsufficient
Relocate the 7.9 W VTX externally—2.5×−12 gAchievable
Fins reach 3.4× while adding 101 g — nearly doubling the assembly mass — and still fall short of 9.2×. That result is what redirects the redesign from incremental surface area to architecture. Moving the video transmitter outside removes 7.9 W of the roughly 11 W load and drops the requirement to 2.5×, which fins can deliver. The redesign target changed because the arithmetic ruled out the alternative, not because the alternative looked unattractive.
05

Outstanding Work

What phases 5 and 6 cover.

Thermal FEA

Resolve internal gradients and component hot spots that the lumped model cannot see. If the FEA disagrees with the hand calculation by more than ~30%, that gap needs explaining before either result is trusted.

Random vibration

Miles' equation for 3-sigma response, then static FEA to verify positive margin against 6061-T6 yield. REQ-STR-003 is currently open.

MIL-STD-810H profile

Extract and justify the Method 514.8 PSD profile for a small multirotor. The excitation band is currently platform-derived only.

Phase 5 redesign

Relocate the video transmitter, add fins to the 2.5× target, and re-run modal — with the lid contact treatment revisited so mode 1 converges.
