01

Why I built it

Writing requirements at Lockheed Martin showed me that hardware is accepted when every requirement has a number and a verification behind it. I wanted to own that whole loop myself, so I picked a part small enough to finish and held it to the same standard.

Where it applies: frequency separation from a rotor order, random vibration margin, heat rejection from a sealed box and a mass budget are the same four constraints on any avionics LRU, payload housing or spacecraft electronics enclosure.

02

Design & process

6061-T6, 93 × 63 × 33 mm, 69.7 g. Flight controller on standoffs above the ESC; video transmitter and 6S capacitor alongside. Zero interferences, 1.00 mm minimum clearance.

The GPS and receiver mount outside — an aluminium box is a Faraday cage and both use integrated antennas. The requirements work caught that before any geometry existed.

03

Structural results

Modal FEA put the first mode at 3,984 Hz, clear of the 87–2,293 Hz prop-passing band. Static stress then ran the 3σ random-vibration load — 94.7 g — separately in three axes.

Governing case, −Z. Peak von Mises 8.47 MPa at a mounting tab hole edge. The in-plane cases came in at 0.58× and 0.32× of this, confirming the out-of-plane axis governs.

Minimum safety factor 32.5 on the housing against 6061-T6 yield — a margin of +27.5 on the MMPDS A-basis allowable.

The FEA came in 37% below my hand calculation, which is the direction I wanted. I had sized the tab by hand at 13.5 MPa using an estimated stress concentration and a 4 mm moment arm; the FE peak is 8.47 MPa because the bolted bore relieves part of the bending. The lid matched within 19%. Both sit inside the ±50% agreement criterion I set beforehand, so the conservative hand value is what stays in the margin table.
04

Thermal results

A six-node Python network sized the problem, then Fusion thermal studies checked it at hover and cruise with 10.85 W of internal load against a 35 °C ambient.

Shell at hover — 63.2 to 71.0 °C.

Components exposed. Flight controller hottest at 71.0 °C, capacitor coolest at 66.0 °C.

Base floor. The 7.9 W video transmitter spreads a clear hot spot through the floor.

The shell agreed within 10%. The components did not — by design. Fusion has no internal air node and bonds every joint perfectly, so it under-predicts the boards by 30–55%. My network carries real joint resistances and an air node, so it stays the conservative model for component margins while the FEA confirms the shell-to-ambient path. Knowing which model to trust for which number was the useful part.

Every component clears its own limit on the sealed box. The internal air requirement does not — closing it took fins, derating the transmitter and two 25 mm fans, which bring air from 73.0 °C to 39.4 °C.

05

Verification status

The one open requirement rests on a number I could not source. The 40 °C internal air limit comes from a flight controller figure with no datasheet behind it. If it is a component rating rather than an ambient one, the anodised box passes with no fins, no fans and no derating. Confirming it is the next thing I do, because it decides whether the redesign is needed at all.
06

Challenges & limitations

Mode 1 never converged

Its shift grew from 3.23% to 3.96% across three meshes instead of shrinking. Modes dominated by the single-body base converged cleanly; every poor one crosses a bonded contact. I carried the lowest of the three frequencies as the conservative value.

Bonded contacts overstate stiffness

Modelling every joint as fully preloaded inflates natural frequencies — the direction that makes the frequency requirement easier. A 2.0× separation factor would not close, so that sensitivity is recorded rather than claimed as margin.

No measured vibration data

No flight data exists for this airframe, so I used the MIL-STD-810H minimum-integrity profile. The standard is explicit that this screens a design, it does not qualify one.

Analysis only, and marginal at that

No test article. The network uses handbook conductances, and the redesign holds by 0.6 K — not robust to those assumptions. Open items are tracked, the datasheet question first.
