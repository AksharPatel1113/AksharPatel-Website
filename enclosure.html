01

Why I built it

Working in systems engineering taught me that hardware earns its place by meeting requirements, not by looking finished. I wanted to run that loop end to end on something I owned completely — so I wrote the requirements first, designed the enclosure to meet them, then ran the analysis that would tell me whether it actually did.

It cleared the structural requirements and missed the thermal one. Finding that out in analysis, before any metal was cut, is exactly what the loop is for.

93 × 63 × 33 mm, 6061-T6, six-fastener lid, four mounting tabs.

Stack bay on the 30.5 mm pattern, divider rib, bay for the VTX and 6S capacitor.

02

Structural: it passes

The props excite 87–2,293 Hz across four harmonics. The requirement is a first mode above 3,440 Hz — 1.5× the top harmonic. It lands at 3,984 Hz, a +0.16 margin, with no mode inside the band.

Ten modes, what moves in each, and which ones converged. Every mode that failed the 2% convergence check has the lid or the capacitor moving — the two parts sitting on bonded contact between separately meshed bodies. That is the tell.

The margin is real but it is an upper bound. Bonded contacts and aluminium standoffs both over-predict stiffness, and mode 1 never converged — its error grew under refinement instead of shrinking. I reported the lowest of three meshes and flagged it rather than quoting the best number.
03

Thermal: it fails

A Python lumped-parameter model run before any FEA, to have an expected answer to check the FEA against. The sealed box misses the 40 °C internal limit in all four flight cases.

Hover is binding. Closing it needs 9.2× the surface area. I sized fins rather than assuming them: they reach 3.4× and add 101 g, more than doubling the 96 g base. Moving the 7.9 W video transmitter outside removes 73% of the load and drops the target to 2.5×.

04

What I took from it

Requirements catch what CAD cannot

Writing the requirements first surfaced that an aluminium box is a Faraday cage. The GPS and receiver moved outside before any geometry existed — and two thermal requirements disappeared with them.

A converged solver is not a converged answer

Mode 1's error grew across three refinements. Modes dominated by a single solid body converged cleanly; modes crossing a bonded contact did not. The pattern pointed at the cause.

Size the obvious fix before rejecting it

Fins were the intuitive answer. Sizing them showed they top out at 3.4× against a 9.2× need, which is what moved the redesign from surface area to architecture.

Log which way an assumption cuts

Seven assumptions, each with its direction of conservatism. Two are non-conservative — and both happen to touch the one requirement that passes.

05

Where this applies

Avionics & payload packaging

Frequency separation from a rotor or engine order, thermal rejection in a sealed LRU, and mass budget — the same three constraints on any airborne electronics box.

Qualification by analysis

Requirements traced to datasheet limits, margins computed against stated allowables, assumptions logged. This is how a V&V package is built when test articles are expensive.

Knowing a model's limits

A non-converging mode and a lumped model that cannot resolve internal gradients are both stated as limits on the result, so anyone reading it knows how far it can be relied on.
