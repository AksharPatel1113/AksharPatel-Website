Problem Statement

Atmospheric re-entry represents one of the most thermally severe environments in aerospace engineering. A Dragon-style blunt-body capsule experiences stagnation temperatures exceeding 1,600 K during peak heating, with heat flux rates that can ablate unprotected surfaces in seconds.

The challenge: accurately predict the coupled aerodynamic heating and structural thermal response across the full re-entry corridor — from hypersonic cruise at Mach 25 down through the subsonic deceleration phase — using ANSYS Fluent for the flow field and ANSYS Mechanical for transient structural thermal analysis, with results validated against the Fay-Riddell stagnation point heating correlation.

An additional objective was to quantify the sensitivity of heat flux predictions to turbulence model selection — a critical modeling decision for flows with strong compressibility effects, shock-boundary layer interaction, and high-temperature gas dynamics.

Methodology

🔬

CFD Setup

ANSYS Fluent density-based solver with ideal gas and Sutherland viscosity. Pressure far-field boundary conditions set for each Mach number in the sweep.

  • Density-based compressible solver
  • Mach 5 → 25 parametric sweep
  • Two turbulence closure models benchmarked
  • Wall heat flux extraction for validation
⬡

Mesh & Validation

Structured mesh with boundary layer refinement to capture the thermal gradient near the ablative heat shield surface. Y+ targeting for each turbulence model.

  • Structured boundary layer mesh
  • Shock-capturing refinement zone
  • Python post-processing pipeline
  • <5% error vs. Fay-Riddell correlation
🌡

Thermal Coupling

CFD-derived heat flux mapped to ANSYS Mechanical as a transient thermal load. TPS material comparison conducted to evaluate time-to-critical-temperature across re-entry phases.

  • CFD → Mechanical heat flux mapping
  • Transient thermal FEA
  • Multiple TPS material comparison
  • Peak temperature tracking vs. time
